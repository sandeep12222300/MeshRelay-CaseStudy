01 · The challenge

What happens when the sender has no connectivity?

A payment instruction cannot always be sent directly to a central service when a device is offline. MeshRelay explores a store-and-forward model where nearby devices can hold and relay packet copies until an online bridge can attempt submission.

01

Offline creation

Represent a payment request as a packet containing fictional sender, receiver, amount, and optional note information.

02

Device-to-device relay

Simulate devices with different connectivity states and relay packet copies through the network.

03

Bridge submission

When a simulated bridge is online, attempt to submit held packets to the backend for demo processing.

05 · Reliability and security

What protects a relayed payment packet?

Offline packets may be copied, delayed, or submitted more than once. MeshRelay’s architecture highlights three controls for handling those risks.

01

Hybrid encryption

AES encrypts the payment payload; RSA protects the AES key for the intended bank-side recipient. This keeps transaction details from being readable to intermediate relay devices in the proposed design.

02

Idempotency

Relays can deliver duplicate copies. An idempotency key and an atomic “already seen?” check (such as putIfAbsent) are intended to ensure repeated submissions do not create repeated settlement effects.

03

Packet TTL

A packet’s time-to-live limits how long it remains eligible for processing. The ingestion step can reject expired packets to reduce the risk of old captured requests being replayed.

Scope: These describe the security and reliability approach represented in the supplied project explanation and architecture. They should not be read as proof of production-grade cryptography, exactly-once delivery, or live banking integration.

07 · Scope

Prototype boundaries

MeshRelay is a demonstration of an offline packet-relay concept, not a production payment system.

Included in the prototype

  • Fictional accounts and balances.
  • Virtual devices with simulated online/offline states.
  • Packet relay and simulated bridge submission.
  • Activity log and demo transaction ledger.

Outside the prototype

  • No live UPI, NPCI, or bank integration.
  • No actual Bluetooth or radio mesh communication.
  • No real-money settlement or production payment guarantees.
  • The static case-study page does not run the Spring Boot application.

Prototype notice: All users, balances, and transactions are fictional. Do not enter real banking credentials.
